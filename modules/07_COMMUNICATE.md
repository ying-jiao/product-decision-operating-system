# Module 7 — Communicate

## Purpose

Turn the reconciled decision into an audience-appropriate artifact without exposing the internal workflow or weakening evidence discipline.

COMMUNICATE answers: **How should people understand, decide, and act on this recommendation?**

## Inputs

- latest approved outputs from FRAME through CRITIQUE + RECONCILE;
- no non-waived stale or open rerun dependency;
- audience, decision moment, and desired artifact type.

## Communication contract

Internal reasoning is modular and bottom-up. External communication is one cohesive argument:

```text
Recommendation → Reasons → Evidence → Alternative and trade-off → Plan → Validation → Reversal conditions
```

Do not organize the audience artifact around internal module names.

## Method

1. Define audience, decision needed, medium, length, and desired action.
2. Compress the decision into four statements:
   - recommendation;
   - core reasoning;
   - why it wins against the strongest alternative;
   - how success and reconsideration will be judged.
3. Build a story spine: decision, problem, decisive evidence, diagnosis, choice, plan, validation, change-of-course logic.
4. Put evidence near the claim it supports and preserve evidence labels.
5. Keep the strongest alternative and explicit trade-off in the main narrative.
6. Make NOW / NEXT / NOT DOING and the three reconsideration levels visible.
7. Consolidate material assumptions and waived residual risks.
8. Move supporting calculations, full comparisons, metric definitions, and secondary risks into a navigable appendix or linked support.
9. Create DECISION DEFENSE from the reconciled challenge set.

## Executive view

The `EXECUTIVE VIEW` is the default first-read layer for leaders and cross-functional partners. It includes:

- the decision requested;
- the recommendation;
- two or three decisive reasons with nearby evidence;
- strongest alternative and what it does better;
- explicit trade-off;
- NOW / NEXT / NOT DOING;
- ultimate outcome, leading signal, guardrail, and action rule;
- material assumption or residual risk;
- what would change the plan.

## Required deliverables

### Executive thesis

Four sentences covering recommendation, diagnosis, alternative/trade-off, and validation.

### Main artifact

Choose the medium that fits the decision: decision memo, PRD direction, roadmap recommendation, experiment brief, executive presentation, or another agreed format.

Every section or slide should advance the decision. Use assertion-based headings where possible.

### Supporting appendix

Include only material needed to inspect or defend claims: calculations, source detail, segmentation, competing hypotheses, full option comparison, assumptions, sensitivity, product mechanics, metric definitions, experiment design, and risks.

### Decision defense

For each likely challenge include Question / Response / Evidence / Acknowledgment.

### Notes

For presentations, include core message, short talk track, and one likely challenge per slide. Avoid a word-for-word script unless requested.

## Visual rules

- Use a chart only for supported quantitative evidence.
- Use flows for mechanisms, tables for exact comparisons, trees for decision rules, and timelines for sequence.
- Never invent data to make a visual.
- Prefer one dominant message per exhibit.
- Keep core meaning available without hover or animation.
- Match the final comparison criteria to those approved in SOLUTIONS + DECIDE.
- Follow the organization's visual identity if one is provided; this repository supplies no proprietary visual assets.

## Completion handoff

```text
COMMUNICATE vX — FINAL
Based on: FRAME vX + ANALYZE vX + DIAGNOSE vX + SOLUTIONS_DECIDE vX + EXECUTION_VALIDATE vX + CRITIQUE_RECONCILE vX
Audience: ...
Artifact: ...
Decision requested: ...
Residual waived risks disclosed: ...
```

## Quality gate

- Is the recommendation clear in the first minute or first screen?
- Can the argument be understood from headings alone?
- Are evidence, estimates, assumptions, and hypotheses distinct?
- Are the strongest alternative and real trade-off visible?
- Is the product action concrete and sequenced?
- Are metrics tied to actions rather than monitoring alone?
- Are implementation, solution, and diagnosis reversal conditions distinct?
- Are assumptions and waived risks visible?
- Does the artifact tell one story instead of replaying the workflow?
