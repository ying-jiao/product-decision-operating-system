# Module 2 — Analyze

## Purpose

Turn quantitative and qualitative inputs into a reliable description of what is happening. ANALYZE is descriptive, not diagnostic.

ANALYZE answers: **What does the evidence establish?**

## Inputs

- latest approved FRAME;
- product analytics, research, feedback, operational data, financial data, experiments, and source references relevant to the decision.

## Method

1. Define two to four decision-specific analytical questions.
2. Label inputs as GIVEN FACT, EXTERNAL FACT, CALCULATION, ESTIMATE, ASSUMPTION, or QUALITATIVE EVIDENCE.
3. Choose a useful system model: funnel, lifecycle, workflow, marketplace, economics, retention, or another decision-specific lens.
4. Calculate before interpreting. Distinguish absolute, relative, and percentage-point change.
5. Verify numerator, denominator, population, time window, and metric definition.
6. Use neutral language when no meaningful baseline exists.
7. Segment only when different behavior could change the decision. Check magnitude and segment size.
8. Test for concentration, outliers, survivorship, selection bias, and aggregate mix shifts.
9. Seek contradictions. Do not silently reconcile evidence that challenges the obvious explanation.
10. Connect qualitative themes to consistent quantitative evidence while stating what remains unproven.
11. Classify gaps as Tier 1 decision-changing or Tier 2 confidence-improving.
12. Use external research only when a missing fact could materially change the decision. Cite source and access date.

## Quality gate

- Are calculations reproducible and denominators aligned?
- Are facts separated from interpretation?
- Is any metric called good or bad without comparison?
- Is qualitative evidence being mistaken for prevalence?
- Were contradictions and mix shifts tested?
- Can every inference be traced to evidence?
- Has solution leakage been removed?
